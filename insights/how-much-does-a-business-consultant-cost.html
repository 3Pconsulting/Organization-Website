How Much Does a Business Consultant Actually Cost?

The honest answer depends entirely on how you’re billed — and most owners never get real numbers before they’re already on a call. Here they are.

3-minute read · Real numbers, before you talk to anyone

Most consultants don’t publish pricing. Here’s why that should worry you, not reassure you.

If a price isn’t published, it usually means it’s negotiable, variable, or dependent on how the conversation goes — not a sign of premium service. Real numbers, so you’re comparing apples to apples before you ever get on a call.

The short version: generalist hourly runs $50–150/hr, certified specialists run $150–300+/hr, and typical retainers run $2,500–12,000/month — all before you know how many hours anything actually takes.

The three ways consultants actually bill

Hourly

$50–150/hr for general business consulting; $150–300+/hr for certified specialists (Lean Six Sigma, Master Black Belt-level credentials). The risk: you don’t know the total until it’s over, and the clock keeps running whether or not the fix is actually working.

Retainer

$2,500–12,000/month depending on scope and access level. The risk: you’re paying whether or not there’s active work that month, and it rarely comes with a defined, guaranteed deliverable.

Fixed-price, defined scope

One problem, one price, known before you start. The risk is almost entirely removed — you know exactly what you’re paying and exactly what you’re getting before you commit a dollar.

Why this matters more than it sounds like it should

Open-ended hourly billing punishes you for a consultant working slowly, and rewards them for it. A fixed price does the opposite — the incentive is to solve it efficiently, because the price doesn’t change either way.
