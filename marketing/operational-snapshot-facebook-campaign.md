# 3P Operational Snapshot — Facebook Campaign Playbook

Permanent source of truth for marketing the 3P Operational Snapshot.

Use this document before creating Facebook posts, advertisements, reels,
carousels, landing-page copy, or future Operational Snapshot campaign concepts.
Do not restart the strategy or replace the core campaign idea unless the owner
explicitly requests a new direction.

## Campaign context

- Primary market: Gainesville, Alachua County, and the surrounding Gainesville-area market.
- Primary traffic source: Facebook, with mobile users expected to make up most traffic.
- Initial budget: approximately $1 per day.
- Funnel strategy: concentrate the budget on one primary offer rather than splitting it across several unrelated offers.
- Primary offer: the free 3P Operational Snapshot.
- Assessment length: approximately 60 seconds for the essential questions, with optional detail available.
- Immediate payoff: the user sees an automated Operational Snapshot built from their answers.
- Human differentiator: a real 3P team member can review the completed Snapshot and identify worthwhile opportunities and next steps.

## Locked campaign concept

### Primary headline

**WHAT IS YOUR BUSINESS COSTING YOU?**

This question is not limited to operating expenses. It asks what weak systems,
owner dependence, scattered information, repeated work, missed follow-up, and
poor visibility may be costing the owner in:

- Time
- Money
- Capacity
- Customers
- Growth
- Personal freedom

The Operational Snapshot is the tool that makes those hidden costs visible.
Marketing should lead with the owner's lived problem, not with consulting jargon
or a generic invitation to "take an assessment."

### Primary call to action

**GET YOUR FREE OPERATIONAL SNAPSHOT**

### Strong supporting line

**Take 60 seconds. See what your business is costing you.**

## Core marketing argument

The owner may believe the answer is more customers, more advertising, another
employee, or more hours. The real problem may be money and capacity already being
lost inside the business.

Potential causes include:

- Repeated administrative work
- Information scattered across multiple places
- Entering the same information into multiple systems
- Missed calls or inconsistent customer follow-up
- Unclear responsibilities and next steps
- Weak visibility into what is profitable
- Work that cannot move without the owner
- Existing customers not being fully served
- Hiring into disorganization instead of fixing the underlying system

The strongest expression of this argument is:

> You may not need to work more or find more customers. You may need to see where
> your business is already costing you time, money, and opportunity.

## Four central promises

Use these consistently across campaign copy and supporting graphics:

1. **Recover owner time**
2. **Keep more of what you earn**
3. **Reduce how much depends on you**
4. **Know what to address first**

These are stronger than vague promises about efficiency, optimization, or growth
because they tell the owner what improves in everyday life.

## Pain-point question bank

These questions came from the development of the Snapshot and should be reused as
individual Facebook posts, opening hooks, reels, carousel slides, polls, or ad
variations.

### Owner time and freedom

- Are you working more than 40 hours a week just to keep the business moving?
- When was the last time you took a real vacation?
- How much of the business stops when you stop?
- Are you working more because the business is growing—or because it is disorganized?
- If you stepped away for one week, what would stop, get missed, or come looking for you?

### Repeated work and scattered information

- Are you entering the same customer or business information into multiple places?
- Is your business information organized in one place—or scattered everywhere?
- How many hours do routine administrative tasks consume every week?
- How often do you search through texts, emails, notes, spreadsheets, or different systems to find the information you need?
- Are you doing work repeatedly because your systems do not communicate with one another?

### Customers, money, and missed opportunity

- Do you need more customers—or are weak systems causing you to lose money from the customers you already have?
- Are missed calls, inconsistent follow-up, or unclear responsibilities costing you customers?
- Could you make more without doing more?
- Could you keep more of what you already earn?
- Are you trying to create more revenue before fixing the places where revenue is already leaking?

### Help, delegation, and growth

- If you had the money to hire help tomorrow, what would you hand off first?
- Would another employee solve the problem—or inherit the same disorganization?
- If you had access to an AI assistant or virtual operations team, what would you want handled first?
- Do you want to grow, remain intentionally small, or simply stop carrying everything yourself?
- What is actually preventing growth: money, time, systems, knowledge, staffing, marketing, or capacity?
- If someone gave you $50,000 for your business tomorrow, would you know where it would create the greatest return?

## Tone and language rules

- Speak in everyday business language.
- Lead with a recognizable problem or question.
- Translate internal terms such as "handoff," "recurring work," and
  "standardization" into situations an everyday business owner immediately understands.
- Do not imply that every business needs more customers.
- Do not promise exact savings or growth before the owner completes the Snapshot.
- Do not make the campaign sound like generic SaaS, software sales, crypto, a
  computerized diagnostic, or traditional stock consulting.
- Do not describe the Snapshot as a long or complicated assessment.
- Do not use "3–5 minutes" in campaign messaging; retain the approved
  **approximately 60 seconds** positioning for the essential Snapshot.
- Emphasize immediate results and the optional human review.

## Funnel message

The campaign should communicate this sequence clearly:

1. A business owner recognizes a familiar pain point.
2. The owner completes the short Operational Snapshot.
3. The live Snapshot builds from the owner's answers.
4. The owner receives immediate insight into time, money, visibility, capacity,
   and opportunity.
5. A real 3P team member can review the business and determine which next steps
   are genuinely worthwhile.

The final position is not "buy more software." It is:

> See the business clearly, understand what deserves attention, and make the
> right improvements first.
